The first form of telephones developed as an evolution of the electrical telegraph, moving from sending coded text to transmitting the human voice. While several inventors contributed to its birth, Alexander Graham Bell is widely credited for the first practical model, receiving a U.S. patent on March 7, 1876.
Mechanism: Bell's initial design utilized a transmitter (microphone) and a receiver (earphone). Sound waves caused a thin iron plate, or diaphragm, to vibrate. These vibrations induced a fluctuating electric current that traveled through a wire to a second instrument, where another diaphragm mirrored the vibrations to recreate the original sound.
